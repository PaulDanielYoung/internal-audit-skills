# Documented Methodology

Maintain a concise account of the organization's documented audit methodology in `METHODOLOGY.md`.

## Authority and evidence

Documented methodology governs audit work. Preserve required versus recommended steps, applicability conditions, scales, thresholds, and exceptions. When a remark or existing summary conflicts with the source, surface the conflict and ask for clarification. An informal practice or preference does not amend the methodology. When sources disagree or their current status is unclear, establish which documented source governs before recording a requirement as settled.

Link each requirement or coherent group to its source document and section or page, with its version or date when available. Use the actual title and accessible path or URL. Reference lengthy procedures or templates and state when a consuming skill must consult them. The source governs if the summary differs.

## Starting structure

Read [METHODOLOGY-TEMPLATE.md](METHODOLOGY-TEMPLATE.md) when creating the document. During explicit workspace setup, copy it even if the manual is unavailable. Otherwise, use its structure as supported content becomes available. Its headings and prompts are organizational aids, not audit requirements.

Organize the populated document around the supplied methodology. Prioritize rules needed for current work, such as engagement planning, risk assessment, scope justification, required deliverables, and review or approval. Add other activities when supported; the template is not a complete audit lifecycle checklist.

## Maintenance

Keep decision rules intact when summarizing. Replace prompts with sourced content, retain a clear account of coverage and material gaps, and remove unused headings when they no longer help. Missing information does not mean that no requirement exists. Request the governing source or clarification rather than filling gaps with generic audit practice.

Update the summary and its source references when a governing document changes. Keep the coverage status partial until the relevant sources have been reviewed; a populated template alone does not establish completeness.
