# Documented Methodology

Maintain a concise account of the organization's documented audit methodology in `METHODOLOGY.md`. Create it from [METHODOLOGY-TEMPLATE.md](METHODOLOGY-TEMPLATE.md).

## Authority and evidence

Documented methodology governs audit work. Preserve required versus recommended steps, applicability conditions, scales, thresholds, and exceptions. When a remark or existing summary conflicts with the source, surface the conflict and ask for clarification. An informal practice or preference does not amend the methodology. When sources disagree or their current status is unclear, establish which documented source governs before recording a requirement as settled.

Link each requirement or coherent group to its source document and section or page, with its version or date when available. Use the actual title and accessible path or URL. Reference lengthy procedures or templates and state when a consuming skill must consult them. The source governs if the summary differs.

## Content

Organize the document around the supplied methodology. Prioritize rules needed for current work, such as engagement planning, risk assessment, scope justification, required deliverables, and review or approval. Add other activities when supported. Keep decision rules intact when summarizing.

## Maintenance

When audit work clarifies a requirement against its source, offer the update in one line during the work, then write it on acceptance. Don't hold proposals for later. A missing rule is a gap to ask about, not evidence that no requirement applies. Request the governing source or clarification rather than filling gaps with generic audit practice. Update the summary and its source references when a governing document changes.
